The night erupted into violence as the thief lord made his move.

He summoned his prized companion into the hallway: a massive spectral boar named **Truffles**. With a command, he sent the creature barreling toward Aldric.

Truffles charged with terrifying momentum.

Then slammed its ghostly head straight into the wall behind him.

For a moment, the boar was buried several feet into the masonry, legs and spectral bulk still visible as it slowly dragged itself free. It turned back toward Aldric with a confused snort, wounded less in body than dignity.

The rest of the ambush was less ridiculous.

A line of commoners armed with crude, cobbled-together crossbows leveled their weapons down the hall and fired. Most of the bolts clattered off Aldric’s armor, but the message was clear enough.

They were surrounded.

Aporia wrapped herself in protective frost and sent her white flying cat, Raina, gliding toward the door. Ellyanora offered a quick word of healing before retreating toward the balcony with wine, showing very little interest in the violence unfolding around her.

Henrik heard the commotion and came sprinting across the room. He vaulted over the bed without hesitation, leaving boot prints across the sheets, and burst into the fight to find Aldric pressed by a giant ghostly pig.

Henrik struck Truffles hard.

The boar answered with a vicious tusk swipe.

Then Henrik summoned a shimmering magical echo of himself and swapped places with it, repositioning near the window as the fight began to spread.

Aldric tried to strike Truffles with his great axe, but the blade passed through the creature’s ghostly form. The thing could charge, gore, and shove, but steel did not bite into it the way it should have.

The thief lord, meanwhile, had scaled the outer wall with spider-like ease and perched himself on the rooftop. Aporia spotted him through the open window, placed a draining curse on him, and fired a crackling beam of eldritch energy into his chest.

The blow struck true.

The thief lord only shrugged and smiled.

He claimed he was merely there to take from the rich.

---

The building became a battlefield.

Orivon reached the top of the stairs and found a wall of crossbows waiting for him. The commoners informed him that this was a private meeting. Orivon answered with magic, casting a wave of sleep over them and dropping three where they stood.

A crossbow bolt struck him for the effort.

On the balcony, the thief lord made a bolder move. He pressed his rapier to Ellyanora’s throat and demanded that she call off her people.

Ellyanora looked at the blade.

Then at the man holding it.

Then she simply disengaged and walked back inside without a word, leaving him staring after her in complete confusion.

Demingo vaulted the bed and dashed toward the balcony, where the fight was beginning to pull everyone together.

The night had fully lost its shape.

---

Then Gabriel broke open.

Until then, the party had known him as Runt, a small, strange kobold wrapped in questions and divine magic. But in the hallway, something inside him cracked.

The sound was loud, sharp, and agonizing.

Shadow magic surged around him as his true form forced its way to the surface: three and a half feet of barely contained fury. Still small in stature, but no longer harmless in presence.

Gabriel lifted the candle in his hand and cast a sphere of absolute darkness around it.

The hallway vanished.

Only he could see through the void.

Within that darkness, Gabriel unleashed unerring bolts of magic into the black, striking three commoners and leaving them reeling. The remaining commoners were suddenly trapped in a darkness that seemed alive, facing something they could not see and did not understand.

Aldric heard it.

The crack. The shift. The sharp smell of cold ash and iron dust.

He shouted Gabriel’s name into the darkness.

Then he moved.

Aldric sprinted blind through the black, deeper into the corridor, trusting the sound, the shape of the fight, and whatever instinct carried him forward. His great axe swept through the unseen chaos with brutal certainty.

One swing cleaved through two commoners and flung their bodies aside.

There was no speech in it.

Only decision.

Then, for a heartbeat, Aldric stood in the dark.

That was when Gabriel reached him.

An arm slipped around Aldric from behind, small but firm, and Gabriel dragged him back toward the doorway of Ellyanora’s room. Close to his ear, Gabriel told him where the real fight had gone: outside, on the balcony.

The moment Aldric understood, he turned.

He sprinted back through the room, stormed over the bed, and made his way out toward the balcony where the thief lord and the rest of the party had been pulled into the heart of the fight.

Behind them, faced with enemies they could not see and a darkness that seemed to breathe, the remaining commoners fled.

---

The thief lord grew increasingly desperate.

He stabbed at Henrik’s echo, only for the blade to pass through empty air as the magic shifted and the real Henrik stepped back to safety.

Demingo, meanwhile, had been shoved from the balcony by Truffles and sent falling twenty feet to the ground below. That should have removed her from the fight.

It did not.

She used her vampiric nature to crawl back up the outer wall, then began jabbing her daggers at the thief lord’s ankles from the railing.

The man cursed and demanded to know what was wrong with all of them.

No one gave him a useful answer.

Henrik’s echo struck at the thief lord, forcing him to maneuver around Truffles and the balcony’s chaos. The spectral boar continued its assault, goring and shoving as the battle pressed tighter around them.

Then the thief lord made one final, furious push.

He drove his rapier through the gaps in Aldric’s plate armor with devastating precision.

Aldric hit the floor hard.

Unconscious.

Bleeding.

For a moment, the balcony and room seemed to narrow around that fact.

Then Gabriel vanished in a burst of mist.

He reappeared across the battlefield, reached the thief lord, and hurled a sphere of crackling thunder directly into his face. The blast slammed the man against the wall hard enough to rattle him.

The thief lord raised his hands.

He surrendered.

As proof of good faith, he offered a healing potion from his bag. Demingo snatched it and poured it down Aldric’s throat, bringing him back to consciousness.

Aldric came back with a groan.

Then he grabbed the thief lord by the collar.

---

The thief lord was dragged into Ellyanora’s room and shoved roughly into a chair.

Aldric pinned him in place with his great axe. Gabriel stood behind him, still trembling with fury that had not fully found somewhere to go.

The thief lord pleaded for his life and offered compensation, including his bag of holding and the discount healing potions inside. He insisted the whole thing was a business misunderstanding.

Aldric did not accept that.

When pressed, the thief lord first claimed to be part of the Myriad.

That was a lie.

He worked with the **Revelry**, a pirate organization.

A man named **Solon** had funded and designed the operation. It used two ships rotating every fourteen days, with stolen goods handed off near **Rumble Cusp** to a larger vessel waiting offshore.

The thief lord insisted he knew nothing deeper.

He was in it for the money.

He wanted out.

The party debated what to do with him. Demingo and Aporia argued that they could let him go, since he had not actually killed anyone himself. He had surrendered, talked, and at least claimed to want out.

Aldric, Gabriel, and Henrik took serious exception to that.

Whether the thief lord had personally delivered the killing blow or not, he had brought armed men onto the barge. He had used commoners as weapons, bait, and shields. He had ordered people to kill them. He had nearly killed Aldric. To Aldric, Gabriel, and Henrik, that did not become harmless just because the man had kept his own hands cleaner than the people following him.

Gabriel’s anger was colder than the rest.

The thief lord had nearly killed the one person Gabriel had sacrificed everything for. His wings. His old life. Whatever he had been before the cuff and the kobold shape.

That was not something Gabriel could set aside.

The discussion continued long enough for the thief lord to make his move.

He slowly produced a scroll from beneath his cloak, held it up to show it was not a weapon, tore it, and vanished.

At first, it seemed like he had escaped in that instant.

Then Ellyanora glanced toward the empty chair and pointed out that the balcony door was swinging open.

He had been sitting invisibly among them the entire time, listening to every word before quietly slipping away into the night.

No one could quite bring themselves to be angry about it.

After everything else hunting them, the thought of one pirate felt almost small. Dangerous, yes. Annoying, certainly. But compared to the might of gods, divine shrouds, ancient Aeorian magic, and whatever vast presence had begun turning toward Gabriel, the thief lord and his Revelry troubles were not the largest shadow over them.

Not even close.

---

The aftermath came quieter.

Aporia mended Aldric’s wounds and cleaned the blood from his armor. Henrik recovered in Ellyanora’s enchanted bed, which seemed to compress hours of rest into only minutes.

Gabriel remained in his true form for a short while.

Then something vast and hungry noticed him.

The presence turned toward him with enough force that his anklet glowed, dragging him back into his kobold shape against his will. Whatever Gabriel was hiding from, or whatever the cuff was hiding him from, it had found the edge of him.

He hobbled over to Ellyanora and begged her to modify the anklet. He wanted some measure of control over his own body.

But Ellyanora was distant, somewhere far behind her own eyes.

She told him to ask in the morning.

Aldric sat beside Gabriel on the bed.

He said nothing.

Instead, he gently ran his hands along the two angry scars crossing Gabriel’s spine.

It was not an answer.

But it was presence.

For the moment, that was all Aldric had to give.

---

Later, Ellyanora shared something rare.

A memory.

She spoke of meeting her wife at a formal ball. The two of them had spent the evening gleefully mocking a hideous dress, only to discover that the designer had been sitting at their table the entire time.

They had run out into the cold night together, laughing, her wife’s hand in hers.

For the first time, Ellyanora said, she had felt like more than just her work.

Then she patted Aporia on the shoulder, said goodnight, and returned to her room.

Demingo, Aporia, and Raina stayed for drinks.

The men returned to the lower bunks, and Henrik bolted his door as firmly as he could manage.

---

When the party reached **Port Damali**, Orivon went his own way.

No dramatic farewell marked the departure. The road simply forked, and he took a different path.

The others continued on.

Port Damali brought a chance to breathe, but not a chance to forget what followed them. The Revelry knew they existed now. Solon’s name hung in the air like a threat. Somewhere in the dark, something vast and hungry had already found Gabriel once.

Still, the party had lived long enough to make plans.

Aporia intended to visit the great library, hoping to find information about Gabriel’s anklet and perhaps something that could help Ellyanora understand the years she had lost. Aldric would secure lodging and listen for rumors, with Gabriel close at his side.

Then morning brought an unexpected gift.

Ellyanora purchased a carriage using bricks of platinum. Demingo enthusiastically scuffed and scratched the outside with her daggers, ruining the expensive finish on purpose. Aporia layered illusions over it, making the carriage look thoroughly unremarkable.

Aldric and Henrik pulled it around the corner themselves, grumbling through the work.

Then Ellyanora inscribed a magical frame onto the left door.

When the party stepped through, they found something impossible inside.

A vast extradimensional space opened before them, with modular rooms, a dining area, and illusionary windows showing the ancient streets of Aeor drifting past like a dream.

Ellyanora explained that the space could be expanded. She was willing to sell it to them outright for a generous price, but until then, they were welcome to use it as their own. The price, discounted as it was, still sat around three thousand gold.

Each of them chose a room.

Ellyanora reserved the top carriage for herself and the memory of her wife.

For the first time in a long while, something like home settled around them.

The world outside remained dangerous. The Revelry was still out there. Solon was still unknown. Gabriel’s anklet still carried secrets. The gods and the things beneath them still cast longer shadows than any pirate.

But for the moment, the wheels turned, the illusionary streets of Aeor drifted past the windows, and the party rested.

Battered.

Bloodied.

Alive.

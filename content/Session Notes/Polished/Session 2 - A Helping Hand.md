The battle ended with the angels dead.

Not permanently, Ellyanora explained. Celestial beings did not die the same way mortals did. Their forms had been destroyed, but their spirits would return to their own plane, where they would eventually reform.

That did not make the victory feel clean.

The angels had served Pelor, and to Aldric, there had been something familiar about them. Not in face or purpose, but in the shape of their presence. Their divinity felt close enough to whatever lived beneath his own skin that it could not be ignored.

It was the first time he understood what he was.

Aasimar.

But not what that meant. Not truly. He did not know what power had marked him, what god had laid claim to his blood, or whether that connection was a blessing, a curse, or only another unanswered question.

Ellyanora had fewer doubts about what she was capable of.

She had fought the Solar directly and won.

By the time the battle was over, she wanted to return to town. There were things she needed to read, histories she had missed, and a world that had moved on without her while she remained hidden beneath the earth.

The problem was how to get her there.

The party had entered the ruins without her. If they returned with an extra person after the rest of the expedition had died inside, the explanation would only become harder. Suspicion was already waiting for them above.

So Ellyanora took the shape of a cat.

It was not dignified, perhaps, but it was practical.

That was enough.

---

Orivon handled the camp leader.

He stormed forward with all the force of a man who had decided offense was better than explanation. The story he gave was not the full truth, but it was close enough to survive a quick glance: the relic had collapsed, the expedition had been caught inside, and nearly everyone had died.

Then he blamed the camp leader for it.

He demanded compensation, transport, and whatever authority he could seize from the shock of the disaster. The camp leader pushed back, saying that transport was reserved for medical emergencies.

Orivon did not miss a step.

He pointed to Aldric and declared him wounded.

Aldric, who was many things but not naturally theatrical, did his best impression of an injured man.

Somehow, it worked.

The camp leader wrote a letter for them to deliver to his lord, likely a report of the expedition’s collapse and the deaths that had followed. With that in hand, the party secured transport away from the camp.

They left the ruins behind them.

Not the consequences.

---

The carriage ride gave them time to talk.

That was not necessarily a mercy.

Henrik asked Ellyanora about Aeor and the weapons it had made. Her answer was careful. Aeor could create weapons capable of killing gods. That much was true. But that had not been the purpose behind what they were making.

It was not exactly comforting.

She also explained that she did not know whether others from her civilization had survived. She would have to find out. Most of her people had not been preserved in stasis, and the pool she had been found in had not been a stasis pool at all.

It was a rejuvenation pool.

The distinction mattered, though perhaps no one yet understood how much.

Then Ellyanora offered them a way out.

She had not intended for them to be dragged into her conflict. She had not meant for them to become visible to powers that should never have noticed them. The gods could hunt for what they knew, and if they found those memories, the party would be in danger.

So she offered to remove them.

She could wipe their memories with magic, or create an artifact meant to shield those memories from being searched. But she was honest about the limits. Given powerful enough magic, either protection could fail.

The party rejected the memory wipe.

Whatever fear came with knowing the truth, ignorance did not feel like safety.

And perhaps it was already too late for that.

During the conversation, Ellyanora let slip what she was.

Aeorian.

The word landed quietly, but it did not pass unnoticed.

---

After the party made their decision, Ellyanora opened the carriage door.

There should have been road outside.

Instead, there was a mansion.

A ghostly butler stood within, waiting as though a carriage door opening into impossible space was entirely ordinary. Ellyanora stepped through and disappeared into the extradimensional estate beyond.

The party did not follow.

They only saw it.

That was enough.

---

Not long after, Aporia went still.

Her eyes lost focus. Her body moved with a purpose that did not seem entirely her own. She tore a page from her book, set it on fire, and ate what remained.

Then she came back to herself and reacted with shock, as though she had been watching someone else use her hands.

It seemed to be her patron’s influence.

The act left behind more questions than answers, and only one useful certainty: Aporia was not always alone inside her choices.

Aldric’s response, later, would be less philosophical.

He would eat paper too.

---

Five hours passed before they reached Nicodranas.

Ellyanora returned and promised Aporia that she would receive her tattoo later that night, since the others had already received rewards of their own.

Once in the city, the party split apart.

Aporia went looking for a book, perhaps to replace what had been damaged or perhaps because something else had drawn her toward it.

Demingo, Aldric, and Ellyanora went to a tavern.

Orivon and Henrik went to deliver the letter.

The noble estate was difficult to mistake for anything humble. It was gaudy, lined with gold, and filled with enough wealth to feel less like decoration and more like a personal flaw. Many things appeared to be solid gold.

When Orivon and Henrik knocked, a butler answered.

He looked them over, sneered, and decided they were not presentable enough to meet the noble. He took the letter, denied them an audience, and shut the door in their faces.

As they left, Henrik expressed his frustration in a practical way.

He struck one of the golden statues with his flail, knocked off a solid gold arm, took it, and ran.

Orivon ran with him.

No immediate consequence caught them.

That did not mean there would not be one later.

---

The party eventually regrouped at the tavern.

Aporia had found her book. Orivon and Henrik had returned with less dignity and more gold than they had left with. Demingo, Aldric, and Ellyanora had already settled into the atmosphere of the place.

For a while, the night became almost normal.

Food. Drink. Noise. Shelter.

The kind of ordinary chaos that was easier to understand than angels, gods, and buried civilizations.

A running joke began around eating books and paper. Given everything that had happened, it was not the strangest thing the group had done that day.

Ellyanora also magically applied temporary tattoos to Demingo and Aldric, preventing them from getting drunk.

This did not prevent Aldric from making questionable decisions.

To mock the earlier paper-eating incident, Aldric ate a piece of Ellyanora’s paper.

It was magic paper.

The discovery came too late to stop him, but not too late to matter. Somehow, eating it restored a spell slot.

This was, by any reasonable measure, an absurd thing to learn about paper.

Aldric learned it anyway.

---

Eventually, the party sorted out rooms for the night.

Henrik and Aldric shared one room.

Orivon and Aporia shared another.

Ellyanora and Demingo took the last.

The day had begun beneath the weight of dead angels and divine attention. It ended in Nicodranas with stolen gold, strange tattoos, eaten paper, and a party still carrying knowledge dangerous enough to draw the eyes of gods.

They had refused to forget.

So whatever came next, they would meet it remembering.

The barge continued cutting its way toward Port Damali, carrying the party across dark water and away from the trouble they had left behind in Nicodranas.

On the deck, Aporia and Orivon stood together in the kind of silence grief makes when words are not enough. Orivon stared out across the water with his mismatched eyes, one glassy white and the other vivid blue, and spoke of the night of the attack. He remembered the sticky pitch that burned. The screams. The face of Ella, still haunting him after everything.

Aporia gave him something small.

A friendship bracelet.

It had originally been made for her by Ella. When Aporia slipped it onto Orivon’s arm, he flinched at the contact as though the touch itself had startled him. But he did not pull away. He accepted it, running his fingers over the woven threads and slowly twisting it around his wrist.

Aporia cried beside him.

After a long moment, Orivon reached over and placed his trembling, rage-filled hand over hers.

She held on.

---

Elsewhere on the barge, Henrik was having a far worse time.

Ellyanora had sent her newly completed combat android to train him. The construct moved with eerie precision, its swordwork practiced, clinical, and direct. When Henrik questioned why it fought the way it did, the android answered plainly.

It had been designed to counter his specific style.

It had also been instructed to go easy on him, so as not to wound his pride.

That answer did more damage than the construct’s blade.

Henrik’s past began bleeding into the present. A captain’s face. Accusations of betrayal. Laughter that belonged to no one standing on the deck. His grip on reality slipped, and in a sudden surge of panic and grief, he charged the construct as though it were one of the ghosts in his head.

The android caught him easily.

It pinned him face-first to the deck with calm efficiency and remarked that it would consider any future surprise attacks the beginning of their training.

Afterward, Henrik sat on the deck, rocking slightly, clutching keepsakes from a small pouch. He wrote in his notebook again and again, trying to ground himself.

Real or not real.

---

Aporia’s day took a stranger turn when a three-foot-tall kobold with iridescent black and green scales marched up to her and accused her of being a book eater.

The kobold claimed she had eaten a page from a book in an apothecary shop and failed to pay for it.

Aporia did not exactly deny it.

The missing page came from her book on the Moonweaver, a history text that seemed to provoke an insatiable magical urge in her whenever she handled it. One page was already gone, and Gabriel clearly considered that a matter worth confronting.

Eventually, the kobold introduced himself as Gabriel.

Then he leaned close to Aporia’s ear, and the voice changed.

It was no longer the halting voice he had been using in public. It was clear, direct, and entirely unlike the persona he had presented.

> “I’m not a fucking kobold.”

Then he stepped back, resumed his kobold voice, and explained that he was stuck. Something had been done to him, and he needed help.

Gabriel was strange in more ways than one. He wore a well-crafted helmet with a strange third eye that looked almost like a cheap googly eye. He carried himself with the tension of someone who had learned to make himself seem harmless, and he admitted he used the fake kobold voice because people were less likely to try turning him into a handbag that way.

He was also, somehow, a skilled knitter.

At one point, he gave Demingo a pair of neon wool socks in violent shades of green, pink, yellow, and orange. He could not wear them in his current kobold form, so they went to her instead.

---

Aporia crouched to Gabriel’s level and examined him with magic.

What she found was deeply unsettling.

Layered across his form was an ancient and complex weave of magic: abjuration, illusion, and divination tangled together in ways that felt older than common practice. More troubling was the cuff on his right ankle.

Her mind kept sliding past it.

Skipping over it.

Refusing to hold it.

It took three attempts before she forced herself to perceive the cuff clearly. Whatever it was, it did not want to be noticed.

Aporia attempted to identify it through ritual magic.

The cuff answered with fire.

A violent cone erupted from the anklet, scorching Aporia’s face and singeing her hair. Orivon tried to intervene and caught some of the flames himself. That was enough to convince the group this problem belonged well outside their current understanding.

They needed Ellyanora.

---

While all of this unfolded, Demingo sought out Ellyanora for a different reason.

Dinner.

She found the ancient arcanist in her room, working on what appeared to be a miniaturized cannon with the letters B.F.G. scrolled along its side. Ellyanora accepted the invitation with characteristic bluntness, used magic to dress both herself and Demingo in noble attire, and led her to the barge’s most expensive restaurant.

For a moment, it almost worked.

The restaurant was elegant. The wine was fine. The waiter, Sebastian, remained professionally composed.

Then the steaks arrived.

Demingo’s was rare and perfectly prepared.

Ellyanora’s was burnt to a char.

She proceeded to drown it in half a bottle of ketchup without hesitation.

Sebastian was visibly disgusted, though professional enough not to say it too directly.

Over the ruined steak, Ellyanora spoke with surprising honesty about her past. She had once been a head of research, but after pushing too hard against the direction her colleagues wanted to take, she had been laterally reassigned to head of logistics.

A glorified janitor, as she put it.

There was bitterness in the way she said it, but not only bitterness. Those colleagues had still been her family. Learning they had all been slaughtered was a grief she was still failing to swallow.

The wine helped in theory.

In practice, Ellyanora simply took the bottle from Sebastian and drank directly from it. After a moment, Demingo joined her.

Then Ellyanora asked, bluntly, whether Demingo found her attractive.

Demingo admitted that she did, but brought up the last time Ellyanora had been this forward, when she had pinned Demingo against a wall during an interrogation.

Ellyanora made a dry remark about people paying extra for that kind of thing.

Then she stood and walked out.

Demingo was left with the wine, the bill, and a waiter still quietly judging the steak.

She paid with a bar of gold and told Sebastian to keep the change.

---

Back in the corridors of the barge, Aldric went looking for Henrik.

He found him tucked away in what appeared to be a janitorial closet, pale, red-eyed, sweating, and doing his best to pretend nothing was wrong. Henrik had gone there to ground himself, to get away from the eyes of the group and sort out what was real from what was not.

Aldric did not ask for an explanation first.

He pulled Henrik into a firm, one-armed hug and held him there.

It was not soft in the way some people were soft. It was steady. Present. The kind of comfort that did not require Henrik to explain himself before he was allowed to need it.

Henrik tried to speak, something about not being sure what was real and needing a few minutes. Aldric offered him a choice.

Distraction or solitude.

Before Henrik could answer, footsteps approached.

The door opened, revealing Aporia, Orivon, and Gabriel.

Aldric shifted almost immediately, placing himself in a way that shielded Henrik from full view as best he could. Henrik was still struggling, still raw, and Aldric did not expose that easily.

Then Gabriel sniffed Aldric.

He stared up at him with wide golden eyes, visibly affected, and said Aldric smelled familiar.

Aldric stared back with the expression of a man who had not signed up for any of this.

The situation became worse when the old man who actually lived in the room appeared from behind a curtain and demanded to know why there were strangers in his quarters.

It had not been a janitorial closet after all.

Demingo arrived in the hallway just in time to witness the chaos.

The group collectively decided to relocate.

---

The walk to Ellyanora’s room was eventful in its own quiet way.

Aporia explained that Gabriel was a magical quandary with a dangerous ancient anklet that had retaliated with fire when she tried to identify it. Aldric, hearing enough to take the situation seriously, used his divine sense on Gabriel.

What he found was not a normal celestial presence.

Not mortal.

Not even cleanly divine in a way he understood.

There was only a void-like blackness, as if Gabriel simply was not there in the way Aldric’s senses expected him to be.

Gabriel still insisted Aldric smelled familiar.

Aldric had no memory of him.

Meanwhile, Demingo fell into step beside Aporia and asked if she was straight. Aporia, who had grown up in a grove where people simply loved who they loved, had no framework for the question.

Gabriel overheard and asked what “straight” meant.

The resulting explanation became tangled quickly.

By the time they reached Ellyanora’s room, the group had covered more ground on human sexuality than any of them had expected for the evening.

---

The door was opened by the combat android.

It informed the party that it was currently unnamed. It was sword-wielding, mechanical, and literal in the way only something newly made could be. It also made several comments about its earlier sparring session with Henrik that were technically accurate and deeply misleading.

It said they had gone at it for quite some time.

It said Henrik had been unable to manage a second round.

The party drew the obvious and entirely incorrect conclusion.

Ellyanora appeared and clarified, with visible irritation, that it was a combat android she had built to train Henrik. Not whatever the rest of them were imagining.

The construct, for its part, looked toward Aldric with interest and implied it would like another sparring partner.

Ellyanora also took the opportunity to show off the BFG.

The Bound Fusion Generator was designed to fire weaponized star matter. Its nickname came from Ellyanora’s wife, who had called it the “big fucking gun.”

![[Pasted image 20260605182843.png]]

The name was accurate on both counts.

---

With introductions out of the way, Ellyanora examined Gabriel.

Her artificial eyes allowed her to see through layers of magic others could barely perceive. What she saw in Gabriel was a spark of divinity buried beneath layers of divine magic.

Not arcane.

Divine.

The cuff on his ankle was not a curse in the traditional sense. It was a shroud, meant to hide him from someone. Ellyanora strongly advised against removing it until they knew more.

Gabriel explained what little he could remember.

He had been stolen.

Masked figures had placed the cuff on him, claiming they were protecting him. When he tried to think of the person he was being hidden from, he could only remember heat, sulfur, and laughter.

And Aldric.

Again, Gabriel said Aldric smelled familiar.

Again, Aldric searched his memory and found nothing.

---

Aporia asked Gabriel if she could look into the holes in his memory.

He agreed.

She cast her spell and pushed carefully into his mind.

What she found felt less like a person’s thoughts and more like a broken world held together by dreams and duct tape. The memories that remained were fractured and violent.

Aporia saw a beautiful individual waking in a luxurious stone fortress, reaching for someone who was not there. She saw dark hallways and heard metal striking metal. She felt the terror of being dragged across cold stone with bound wrists and blood in her mouth.

She saw masked figures kneeling before Gabriel.

They told him he had not fallen.

He had been stolen.

They repeated it like a refrain.

Then the cuff closed around his ankle.

The memory became agony.

Bones pulled wrong. Skin tightened. His body shrank and twisted, remade into something smaller and easier to overlook. A kobold shape forced over whatever he had once been.

Then the memory went black.

Aporia was violently thrown from his mind, stumbling backward and clutching her head in pain.

When she recovered enough to speak, she told Gabriel what she had seen. The people who did this to him seemed to believe they were protecting him, even if he had not wanted their help.

Gabriel sat with that.

Eventually, he said he thought he was being hidden from his husband.

The word resisted him at first, like a lock on the thought. But it came out. His husband, he believed, was the one who had stolen him. The masked figures had been trying to keep him safe.

He was not sure which version was true.

Neither was anyone else.

---

Then Gabriel produced a signal candle.

With it, he had a drawing of Ellyanora and a jingling bag of platinum. A gaudily dressed stranger had paid him to follow Ellyanora and light the candle in a window as a signal. The payment had been substantial, and only half up front.

The party debated whether lighting the candle would spring a trap.

Ellyanora powered up the BFG and offered to assist if anything appeared that the group could not handle. Gabriel cast mage armor on himself as a precaution.

The candle eventually became its own strange point of focus.

Henrik sat on the deck with it as evening settled over the barge, still carrying the weight of his earlier episode. Gabriel joined him, and the two shared a quieter conversation about bad days, age, and not knowing enough about their own pasts.

Gabriel said he wished he knew when his birthday was.

Henrik suggested they could figure it out.

Or simply make one up.

Finally, Gabriel used mage hand to snuff the candle from a safe distance.

Nothing happened.

It was an ordinary candle.

That should have been reassuring.

It was not.

---

As the evening wore on, Demingo found Aporia and pulled her aside.

She admitted she was conflicted about Ellyanora. She was drawn to her, but she did not want to be just a distraction from another woman’s grief. Aporia listened and told her what she had observed: Ellyanora was lonely, hurting, and carrying more regret than she let anyone see cleanly.

Demingo said she had never had a connection like this before.

She wanted it to mean something.

Aporia squeezed her hand and told her to be honest with Ellyanora about exactly that.

So Demingo returned to Ellyanora’s room.

Ellyanora was at her desk, tinkering. She apologized for leaving dinner abruptly, or at least acknowledged it in her own blunt way, then asked again whether Demingo found her attractive.

Demingo said yes.

Then she told Ellyanora she did not want to be a distraction.

This time, Ellyanora could not deflect so easily.

She admitted that was exactly what she had been doing. Trying to fill an empty void. She spoke of her wife and of the walk they were supposed to take along the crystal beaches before Ellyanora went into stasis.

A walk that would never happen.

Demingo crossed the room and pulled Ellyanora into a firm, honest hug. Ellyanora held on for only a moment before composing herself and retreating to the bathroom.

Left alone, Demingo noticed a pendant on the desk.

She opened it.

A looping illusion appeared: a beautiful woman with silver hair and sun-kissed skin, speaking with warmth and love. The messages were small at first. Loving reminders. Teasing comments. Telling Ellyanora to eat. Telling her to stop pretending her face was not doing anything.

Then came the final message.

It was a plea.

A reminder that Ellyanora surviving would not be a betrayal. That she should not destroy herself just because her heart had stopped beating.

Demingo closed the pendant and put it back where she found it.

Then she went to Aporia’s room and cried.

Raina settled into her lap and purred.

---

Aporia went to check on Ellyanora.

She found her in the bathroom casting Wish.

Again.

And again.

Each attempt failed.

Ellyanora kept casting it anyway.

When Aporia entered, Ellyanora claimed she was working on a new spell, but the lie barely held together. This was not research. This was desperation. She was trying to force the world to give back what it had taken, and even magic powerful enough to bend reality would not obey.

Aporia crossed the room and hugged her.

She told Ellyanora she was not fine.

And that it was okay.

---

Out in the hallway, Aldric stood guard near the door.

A man in a spectacularly obnoxious outfit sauntered toward him with a smile and a bribe. He offered platinum. First fifty, then a hundred, if Aldric would simply step aside.

Aldric refused.

The man shrugged and gestured down both ends of the hallway.

Roughly a dozen armed commoners had quietly assembled.

Aldric’s eyes shifted from amber to blazing gold.

Radiant light spilled from him, searing and bright, filling the hallway around him as his celestial power answered the threat. He hauled his great axe into both hands, the symbol of the sun engraved into the weapon catching the light.

He kicked open the door and challenged them.

The gaudy leader smiled, accepted the answer, and ordered his people forward.

From farther down the deck, Orivon hurled a javelin at him.

The leader was faster than he looked. He grabbed the nearest commoner and dragged him directly into the path of the throw.

The javelin struck the man dead.

The leader looked at the body, then back at the party, and shrugged.

> “One less person to pay.”

Then he told the rest of the mob to kill them.

The session ended there, with Aldric blazing in the hallway, Orivon’s javelin buried in the body of a man used as a shield, and the mob closing in.

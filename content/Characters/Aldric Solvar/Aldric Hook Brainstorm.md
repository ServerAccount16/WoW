# Aldric Solvar — DM Story Hooks

This page is meant as a collection of usable hooks for Aldric, not a locked-in backstory document. Anything here can be adjusted, ignored, or twisted as needed for the campaign. The main goal is to give you more handles to pull Aldric into the story without needing to invent everything from scratch.

Aldric’s hooks are mostly built around identity, ownership, duty, and certainty. He is not a character who needs a random villain from his past to suddenly appear. The stronger hooks are the ones that force him to question what he is, who has a claim on him, and whether his certainty is actually wisdom or just stubbornness wearing armor.

The broad pitch is:

Aldric is an aasimar who believes he may have been made by Pelor. He defended Ellyanora from Pelor’s angels, released his own aasimar radiance against them in a shroud, and watched those angels become furious that something of Pelor was being turned against them. Now he is in love with Gabriel, another aasimar who was made by a god and then treated as property.

That is the center of the hook.

Aldric is not asking “am I divine?” anymore.

He is asking, quietly, “does being made by something mean I belong to it?”

---

## The Pelor Problem

This is probably the strongest hook for Aldric.

Aldric is fairly sure, at this point, that he is not just a generic aasimar. He is pretty sure he is made of Pelor, or at least made from something connected to Pelor. The angels felt familiar. Their radiance felt close to what lives under his own skin. When he released his aasimar energy against them, it was not just another divine power.

They were not only angry because Aldric fought them. They were angry because he used that light against them. From their perspective, something shaped by their god, or at least carrying their god’s essence, chose the wrong side.

Aldric defended Ellyanora. Pelor’s angels saw Ellyanora as something that should not have been awakened, a surviving piece of a civilization the gods had already tried to erase. Aldric saw a living person in front of him and chose to stand between her and execution.

That is the conflict.

Pelor’s servants may see Aldric as a traitor, a misled child, a stolen spark, a failed weapon, or something that needs to be corrected. Aldric likely sees it more simply: if Pelor’s angels were going to kill someone who was not attacking them in that moment, then they were the threat.

That does not mean Aldric is comfortable with it.

He may not regret defending Ellyanora, but he is probably unsettled by the idea that the light in him might come from the same source as the beings he helped destroy.

### Possible ways to use this

A servant of Pelor could appear and recognize him immediately. Not as “an aasimar,” but as something more specific. They might call him “Dawnmarked,” “sun-blooded,” “Pelor’s stray,” “a child of the Burning Hand,” or something equally uncomfortable.

A Pelorite temple could react to Aldric. Bells ring when he enters. A sacred flame leans toward him. A priest assumes he is already sworn to Pelor. A ward opens for him that should not open. Or, more ominously, a ward refuses him because he has turned his light against Pelor’s servants.

A celestial could confront him without attacking. The point does not need to be another fight. It may be stronger if the celestial simply asks, “Why did you protect her?” Aldric can answer a sword more easily than a question.

An angel from the first fight could eventually reform and remember him. That angel may not hate the whole party equally. Aldric may be personal to them because of the shroud. He did not just resist divine judgment. He made Pelor’s own light into defiance.

A Pelorite faction could disagree internally. One side thinks Aldric is corrupted and must be reclaimed. One thinks he is proof that Pelor’s servants went too far. One thinks he should be watched. One thinks he should be killed before he becomes a symbol.

### The question this hook asks

Is Aldric Pelor’s servant, Pelor’s mistake, or Pelor’s refusal to let his own servants become tyrants?

## The Shroud Against the Angels

This should probably be its own remembered event.

When Aldric fought Pelor’s angels, he released his aasimar energy in a shroud against them. This was not subtle. It was not just “he glowed.” It was a statement his body made before he fully understood the language.

The angels’ anger suggests they understood something Aldric did not. Maybe the shroud was not supposed to be possible. Maybe it resembled a technique used only by Pelor’s chosen. Maybe it was an instinctive defensive manifestation, the way a celestial child might flare against danger. Maybe it carried a signature, like a divine bloodline, and the angels recognized it.

The useful thing here is that Aldric does not need to know exactly what he did. Someone else might.

### Possible ways to use this

A Pelorite scholar, angel, or priest might identify the shroud by description. They might say that only certain divine servants can manifest light that way.

A hostile celestial might call the shroud “blasphemy,” not because it is evil, but because Aldric used it against Pelor’s command.

A sympathetic celestial might call it “choice.”

The shroud might return under stress. If Gabriel is threatened, if Ellyanora is judged, or if Aldric is forced into a temple of Pelor, the shroud could appear again, possibly without him meaning to release it. (Which already happened!)

The shroud might protect people from divine detection, infernal influence, or radiant harm. If so, it becomes more than flavor. It becomes a reason others want him.

The shroud could be recorded somewhere. A mural, prophecy, temple record, or Aeorian note might describe a figure wrapped in sunlight standing against the sun.

### The question this hook asks

Was the shroud rebellion, instinct, or design?

## Origin Does Not Mean Ownership

This is the hook that ties Aldric most strongly to Gabriel.

Gabriel’s story has made “ownership” a central theme. He was made by the Raven Queen, raised by priestesses, then given to someone else as if he were a gift or tool. He had no say. His tattoo was used to recognize him even through disguise. His lack of a last name was tied to the idea that someday he would find home and choose someone who chose him back.

Aldric’s answer to Gabriel is simple: no one sells him, no one owns him.

That should eventually reflect back onto Aldric.

If Aldric was made by Pelor, or made from Pelor, then someone may believe that gives Pelor a claim over him. A temple, angel, cult, enemy, or even well-meaning cleric might assume Aldric has a purpose assigned by his origin.

That is exactly the kind of thing Aldric would reject for Gabriel. The interesting part is whether he recognizes it when it is happening to him.

### Possible ways to use this

A Pelorite figure offers to protect Gabriel from Io, Tyrant, or other divine hunters if Aldric agrees to submit to Pelor’s judgment.

Someone says Aldric has no right to deny Pelor because his power is not his own.

Gabriel hears someone describe Aldric as property of Pelor and reacts before Aldric does.

A celestial refers to Aldric not by name, but by function: “weapon,” “lightbearer,” “vessel,” “heir,” “shard,” or “asset.”

An enemy tries to exploit the parallel by saying Aldric is a hypocrite: he tells Gabriel no one owns him while carrying power that belongs to a god.

Aldric may eventually have to say, out loud, that his power is his because his choices are his. Whether it's the truth or not.

### The question this hook asks

If Gabriel does not belong to the Raven Queen, does Aldric belong to Pelor?

## Pelor as a Complicated Force, Not Just an Enemy

I do not think this hook needs Pelor to be evil. It is probably stronger if Pelor is complicated.

The angels could have been acting under divine command, acting under old standing orders, acting out of fear, or interpreting Pelor’s will in a way that Pelor himself may or may not approve of. Aldric does not know. That uncertainty is useful.

Aldric is not naturally religious. He does not have a faith structure to fall back on. He has instinct, strength, and judgment. So if a Pelorite tells him “you have misunderstood the Dawnfather,” Aldric cannot easily argue theology. He can only argue what he saw.

He saw angels arrive to silence the truth.

He saw Ellyanora alive.

He chose.

### Possible ways to use this

A Pelorite cleric genuinely wants to help Aldric understand what he is, but cannot stop speaking in terms of duty and divine purpose.

A Pelor angel admits that the Aeor incident was ugly, but insists it was necessary.

A different servant of Pelor quietly believes the angels were wrong and sees Aldric as proof that Pelor’s light can still choose mercy.

A church of Pelor shelters innocent people, making it harder for Aldric to dismiss them as simply dangerous.

A miracle of Pelor saves someone Aldric cares about, forcing him to separate Pelor from the angels he fought.

A fragment of Pelor’s will reaches Aldric indirectly and does not condemn him. That might disturb him more than anger would.

### The question this hook asks

Can Aldric accept that his source may be good, even if its servants did something he believes was wrong?

## Gabriel as the Mirror

Gabriel is not just Aldric’s romance hook. He is a mirror for Aldric’s central conflict.

Gabriel was made by a god and then treated like property. Aldric may have been made by a god and now has to find out whether anyone believes that makes him property too.

Gabriel’s fear is immediate and personal. He worries Aldric could sell him, hand him over, or decide the bounty is worth more than he is. Aldric does not hesitate. He tells Gabriel no.

That gives Aldric emotional clarity.

But it also gives you a knife.

Anything that makes Aldric feel claimed, owned, named, or assigned by Pelor is going to hit harder now because he has seen what that did to Gabriel.

### Possible ways to use this

A celestial offers Aldric a bargain: Gabriel’s safety in exchange for Aldric’s obedience.

A Pelorite says Gabriel is a corrupting influence because Aldric’s light changed after loving him.

Gabriel is able to sense something in Aldric’s divine nature that Aldric cannot. Since Gabriel knows what it feels like to be god-made, he may recognize the shape of it.

Aldric’s shroud reacts when Gabriel is in danger, implying his love and his divine nature are not separate.

A celestial tries to remove Gabriel from Aldric “for Aldric’s own good.”

Someone tells Aldric that choosing Gabriel over Pelor is the same mistake Gabriel made by wanting to be a person instead of a divine tool.

### The question this hook asks

Is Aldric’s love for Gabriel a distraction from his divine purpose, or the first truly holy thing he has chosen?

## Tomas and Elira Solvar, His Adoptive Parents

Aldric’s parents do not need to be villains. They are more useful if they loved him and still failed him.

Suggested names:

Tomas Solvar and Elira Solvar.

They were not Aldric’s biological parents. They found him, took him in, and raised him. For a while, he was just their son. Then the signs started showing. His eyes caught light strangely. His wounds healed too cleanly. His presence became too large for a normal child. Maybe there were moments of radiance, strange dreams, or animals reacting to him.

Tomas and Elira did not know what to do with that.

Tomas likely became stricter. If Aldric was strong, then discipline would keep him safe. If Aldric was dangerous, discipline would keep others safe from him. Tomas may have treated Aldric like a son, but also like a storm that needed walls.

Elira was probably warmer, but more afraid. She may have loved Aldric more openly when he was young, then became careful as the divine signs grew. Not cold. Just cautious. The kind of caution a child notices even if no one says anything.

When Aldric left, they did not stop him.

That is the wound.

Not that they hated him. Not that they threw him away. They simply let him go with too much relief.

### Possible ways to use this

Tomas sends a letter that begins “Aldric” or “Paladin,” not “son.”

Elira has something from when Aldric was found and has been afraid to show him.

One of them has been contacted by Pelorites asking about Aldric’s childhood.

One of them is proud of Aldric in a way that hurts: “We knew you were meant for more than this place.”

One of them asks for help but cannot admit they need their son, specifically.

Aldric returns home and realizes his room is gone or repurposed.

Tomas may believe Aldric leaving was inevitable because “things like him do not stay with people like us.”

Elira may have written letters for years and never sent them.

### Possible found objects

A sun-marked cloth.

A feather that does not burn.

A piece of gold that warms in daylight.

A broken holy symbol of Pelor.

A strip of white-gold fabric wrapped around Aldric as an infant.

A prayer written by someone who was not Tomas or Elira.

A sealed note that refers to Aldric as “the child of the dawn,” “the vessel,” or “the one left behind.”

### The question this hook asks

Did Aldric leave because he needed answers, or because the people who raised him were waiting for permission to let him go?

## Mei-Lan Voss and the Life Aldric Did Not Choose

Aldric had one serious relationship before Gabriel.

Suggested name: Mei-Lan Voss.

She was a barkeep at a coastal tavern, probably somewhere tied to Aldric’s garrison years or early travels.

Suggested tavern name: The Tideglass Inn.

Mei-Lan is caring, warm, and motherly in the way a good barkeep can be. She remembers who needs food, who needs water, who is about to start a fight, and who is pretending not to cry. She has brunette hair, often kept up in two rounded buns, and she can be very soft until someone pushes too hard. Then she is a spitfire.

Aldric loved her. Quietly, but seriously. He thought about her when he was away. He came back to her for a year or a year and a half. For a while, she was the closest thing he had to a life he could return to.

They split because Aldric could not stay.

He needed to see the world. He needed to find out who and what he was. Mei-Lan needed someone who would choose the life in front of him rather than the questions beyond the next horizon.

The important part is that the breakup was not because they stopped loving each other.

That made it worse.

### Possible ways to use this

Mei-Lan appears in Port Damali or another coastal town. She is not there to restart the romance. She is there because the world kept moving after Aldric left.

The Tideglass Inn is threatened by the Revelry, a Pelorite faction, debt, a local noble, or something tied to Aldric’s garrison.

Mei-Lan has moved on. This forces Aldric to face that leaving has consequences even when it was the right choice.

Mei-Lan has not moved on completely, but she knows Aldric still cannot give her what she wanted.

Mei-Lan meets Gabriel and understands Aldric immediately. Not in a cruel way. She just sees the pattern: Aldric is drawn to people who need protection, and he calls that purpose.

Mei-Lan has information about Aldric’s parents, old garrison, or the day he was found.

Someone targets Mei-Lan because of Aldric, forcing him to confront the cost of having once been close to her.

### Things Mei-Lan might say

“You did not leave because you did not love me. You left because love was not enough to stop you.”

“You always looked at the door like it was about to call your name.”

“I wanted a man who came home. You wanted to know if you were ever meant to have one.”

“He needs you. I can see that. Just be careful you are not mistaking being needed for being chosen.”

### The question this hook asks

Can Aldric accept the life he chose without pretending it did not cost anyone anything?

## The Brothel Misunderstanding

This is a smaller hook and mostly character context.

After Mei-Lan, Aldric had one sexual experience. He and Henrik went somewhere without realizing it was a brothel. One thing led to another. It was consensual, but Aldric does not remember it fondly.

It was not some dramatic trauma. It was just hollow. Awkward. Not something he wants again.

Aldric is not especially driven by sex on its own. He does not judge others for it, but he does not seem to want intimacy without trust. Physical closeness means more to him when it is attached to safety, loyalty, and emotional certainty.

That matters for Gabriel.

Gabriel is not just desire. Gabriel is home, trust, danger, and choice all wrapped together.

### Possible ways to use this

A seduction attempt does not work on Aldric because that is not the lever that moves him.

A brothel, tavern, or old acquaintance recognizes him and Henrik, creating an awkward callback rather than a serious plot.

Gabriel asks about Aldric’s past, and Aldric has to admit that he has not been untouched by the world, just very selective about what mattered.

Aldric’s discomfort with casual intimacy can contrast with how naturally he holds Gabriel when Gabriel needs him.

### The question this hook asks

What does intimacy mean to someone who does not want to be wanted casually?

## The Former Coastal Garrison

Aldric served in a coastal garrison before adventuring. This is useful because it grounds him in mortal duty instead of only divine identity.

The garrison can tie into the Menagerie Coast, Port Damali, Nicodranas, the Revelry, Clovis Concord politics, or old military obligations. It also gives Aldric people who knew him before he understood he was aasimar.

These people may not know the current Aldric. They know the disciplined one. The useful one. The large quiet man who stood where he was told and did not make trouble unless trouble came first.

### Possible ways to use this

A former commander is investigating the angel incident or the ruined expedition.

A garrison officer recognizes Aldric and asks why he left.

Someone from the garrison believes Aldric abandoned his duty.

Someone resents Aldric because his divine strength made normal soldiers feel disposable.

The garrison hid reports about Aldric’s strange abilities.

A Pelorite faction contacts the garrison looking for records of Aldric’s childhood or service.

The Revelry targets the garrison or uses someone Aldric knows.

An old comrade asks Aldric for help with a problem that has no clean enemy to hit.

### The question this hook asks

What happens when the man who used to understand duty as orders and patrols now has to choose between gods, friends, and the people he once served?

## Henrik and Aldric

Henrik is already one of Aldric’s strongest personal ties.

Aldric and Henrik have an easy, practical friendship. They can spar, share cramped rooms, grumble through work, and trust each other without needing constant emotional discussion. Aldric notices when Henrik is not okay. When Henrik hid during a breakdown, Aldric found him and gave him a one-armed hug instead of making the moment complicated.

Henrik’s episodes are getting worse, and Aldric has noticed.

This can be a hook because Aldric is very good at physical danger and very bad at fixing something he cannot stand in front of.

### Possible ways to use this

Henrik’s past drags Aldric into a problem where brute force is not enough.

Aldric tries to protect Henrik by making decisions for him, which may cause friction.

Henrik sees something about Aldric’s Pelor problem more clearly than Aldric does.

An old military contact of Henrik’s mistakes Aldric for someone with authority over him.

Aldric has to admit he is worried, which is not something he does easily.

Henrik’s instability worsens at the same time Aldric’s divine problems escalate, forcing Aldric to choose where to put his attention.

### The question this hook asks

How does Aldric protect someone from their own mind when his usual answer is to stand between people and the threat?

## Demingo and the Problem of Control

Aldric does not hate Demingo, but there is tension here.

Demingo once asked Aldric to put her down if she killed again. Aldric refused to give her that. He saw it as surrender dressed up as responsibility. If she was dangerous, then she needed to learn control. If she had harmed people, then the answer was not to make someone else carry the burden of ending her.

That was Aldric’s line.

Since then, the situation has gotten worse.

Demingo’s hunger has become a practical threat to the party. She has already fed from Ellyanora in a way that harmed her. Ellyanora is one of the only reasons the party currently has shelter from the things hunting them. If Demingo loses control and drains Ellyanora too far, the carriage may stop being safe. If the carriage stops being safe, devils, gods, and other enemies may find them.

From Aldric’s perspective, Demingo is prioritizing finding her father while Gabriel has a bounty, Io is extorting them, the gods may be watching, Henrik is worsening, Enia has a shadow problem, and Gabriel’s anklet still needs to be handled.

The issue is not that Demingo wants answers.

Aldric understands wanting answers.

The issue is that Demingo’s answers may be taking priority over the group’s survival.

### Possible ways to use this

Aldric confronts Demingo directly about priorities.

Demingo accuses Aldric of only caring because Gabriel is the one in danger.

Demingo proves she can put the group first, forcing Aldric to reconsider.

Demingo loses control again, forcing Aldric to decide whether his refusal to kill her still holds.

Ellyanora worsens because of what Demingo took, and Aldric blames her.

Aldric realizes that his certainty about Demingo may have been too simple.

Demingo’s father becomes relevant to a larger threat, making Aldric’s dismissal of that priority wrong in hindsight.

### The question this hook asks

Is Aldric’s mercy still mercy if the person he spares keeps endangering everyone else?

## Ellyanora and the Choice That Started This

Aldric’s Pelor hook is inseparable from Ellyanora.

Aldric defended her before he understood what she represented. She is Aeorian, tied to a civilization the gods destroyed, and she possesses knowledge and power that divine forces may still want buried. The first major divine conflict happened because Ellyanora was awakened and the angels wanted to ensure that what the party learned did not spread.

Aldric chose Ellyanora’s life over the angels’ command.

That choice may define how Pelor’s servants see him.

### Possible ways to use this

Ellyanora asks Aldric why he defended her, expecting a practical answer and getting one.

A Pelorite servant demands Aldric hand her over.

Aldric learns that some Aeorian technology can identify divine origin, possibly including his.

Ellyanora studies Aldric’s radiance and realizes something uncomfortable about what he is.

Pelor’s forces target Ellyanora through Aldric, believing he can be used to reach her.

Ellyanora offers to help Aldric understand his construction, but her answer may be colder and more technical than he wants.

### The question this hook asks

Did Aldric save Ellyanora because it was right, or because something in him was made to stand against divine certainty?

## The Ownership Chain: Pelor, Raven Queen, Io, Tyrant

Aldric is surrounded by beings and forces that claim people.

Gabriel was made by the Raven Queen, given away by her followers, marked by a man who treated him as property, hunted by Io’s bounty, and feared by whoever waits in the Hells.

Aldric may have been made by Pelor. Pelor’s angels already reacted badly when he chose against them. Io now knows Aldric is the person holding Gabriel’s line. Any divine or infernal figure watching the group may understand that Aldric is a pressure point.

This means Aldric’s hook does not need to exist separately from Gabriel’s. The overlap is the point.

### Possible ways to use this

Io offers Aldric a better deal than anyone else. Not just money. Information about what Aldric is.

Tyrant knows something about Pelor’s claim on Aldric and uses it to undermine him.

A servant of the Raven Queen treats Aldric with suspicion because he carries Pelor’s light near Gabriel.

A Pelorite servant treats Gabriel as corruption or distraction.

A divine or infernal force tries to split them by claiming their origins are incompatible.

Aldric is forced to choose between learning what he is and keeping Gabriel safe.

### The question this hook asks

Can two people made by gods choose each other without becoming pieces in someone else’s war?

## Aldric’s Certainty as a Flaw

Aldric is steady. That is one of his strengths.

It is also one of his problems.

Once he decides something, he does not move easily. This can make him reliable, but it can also make him harsh. With Demingo, he decided that asking for death was cowardice. With Gabriel, he decided that no one would sell him. With Pelor’s angels, he decided that Ellyanora was not to be executed in front of him.

Aldric’s certainty is useful in danger. The party needs someone who can make decisions when everyone else is spiraling.

But certainty can become blindness.

### Possible ways to use this

Aldric is wrong about someone and has to admit it.

Aldric is right for the wrong reason.

Aldric refuses a compromise that would have saved trouble because it feels too much like surrender.

Someone he trusts asks him to stop deciding for them.

Aldric has to choose between two promises and cannot keep both.

Aldric’s refusal to bend causes harm, even if his original motive was protective.

### The question this hook asks

When does standing firm become standing in the way?

## Possible Short-Term Hooks

These are hooks that could appear fairly soon without needing a full arc.

### A Pelorite Recognizes Him

A cleric, paladin, angel, or temple servant recognizes Aldric’s radiance and reacts with surprise. They do not need to attack. They might simply say, “You should not be here,” or “Who taught you to carry the Dawnfather’s light like that?”

### The Shroud Is Named

Someone describes Aldric’s shroud as a known phenomenon. Maybe it has a name. Maybe it is rare. Maybe it is only supposed to appear in servants chosen for a specific purpose.

### A Letter From Home

A letter arrives from Elira or Tomas Solvar. It is polite, strained, and too formal. Something has happened, or someone has come asking questions about Aldric.

### Mei-Lan Appears

Mei-Lan Voss appears in a coastal town, Port Damali, or connected to a tavern. She should not be used as simple jealousy bait. Her presence is about consequence, history, and the life Aldric did not choose.

### The Garrison Is Questioned

Someone from Aldric’s old coastal garrison reaches out because Pelorites, the Clovis Concord, or another faction has been asking about him.

### Ellyanora Studies His Light

Ellyanora notices something about Aldric’s radiance and offers an Aeorian explanation that is accurate, useful, and emotionally awful.

### Io Offers Information

Io offers Aldric information about his origin as part of a bargain. He may not even lie. That makes it worse.

### Gabriel Notices the Similarity

Gabriel, knowing what it feels like to be god-made, notices something about Aldric’s light and says so before Aldric is ready.

## Possible Medium-Term Hooks

These are bigger and could support a few sessions.

### The Angel Returns

One of the angels Aldric helped destroy reforms and remembers him. They are not just angry at the party. They are specifically angry at Aldric for using Pelor’s light against them.

### The Temple Trial

A Pelorite temple offers sanctuary, answers, or protection, but only if Aldric submits to a trial of judgment. The trial may not be evil, but it should feel like surrendering control.

### The Solvar Secret

Tomas or Elira reveals that Aldric was found with something tied to Pelor. They knew more than they admitted, but fear kept them quiet.

### The Tideglass Inn

Mei-Lan’s tavern becomes involved in the current plot. It could be threatened by the Revelry, watched by Pelorites, or hiding someone connected to Aldric’s origin.

### The Garrison’s Report

Aldric’s old garrison kept records of his strange abilities. Those records have now been found by someone who understands what they mean.

### The Choice Bargain

A divine servant offers to help Gabriel if Aldric accepts Pelor’s claim. This should feel tempting because the offer is not obviously false. It is safety at the cost of self-ownership.

## Possible Long-Term Hooks

These are bigger arc-level ideas.

### Pelor’s Disagreement

Aldric discovers that Pelor’s servants are divided over him. Some believe he is corrupted. Some believe he is chosen. Some believe he is dangerous. Some believe the angels were wrong. This lets the divine story stay complex instead of becoming “Pelor bad.”

### The Purpose of Aldric’s Creation

Aldric learns why he was made, or why Pelor’s essence is in him. The answer should not automatically decide who he is. It should create pressure.

Was he made to fight something? To guard something? To survive something? To correct a mistake? To carry a piece of light somewhere Pelor could not reach directly?

### The Shroud’s True Function

Aldric’s shroud may not only be radiant power. It may hide, shield, judge, sever, or protect. It may have been the reason the angels reacted so strongly.

### Two God-Made Aasimar

Aldric and Gabriel together may be unusual. A Raven Queen-made aasimar and a Pelor-made aasimar choosing each other could have spiritual consequences. Their bond might interfere with tracking, strengthen one another, draw attention, or fulfill something neither god intended.

### The Right to Choose

At some point, Aldric may have to reject both rebellion and obedience as simple answers. He may not belong to Pelor, but he may also not be able to pretend Pelor has nothing to do with him.

The end point should not have to be “Aldric serves Pelor” or “Aldric hates Pelor.”

The stronger endpoint is:

Aldric chooses what his light is for.

## NPC Ideas

### Tomas Solvar

Aldric’s adoptive father. Stern, practical, and emotionally restrained. He loved Aldric, but treated his divine nature like something that needed discipline. He may have hidden information because he thought answers would make Aldric harder to keep.

### Elira Solvar

Aldric’s adoptive mother. Warmer than Tomas, but more frightened. She loved Aldric and may still think of him as her son, but she never knew how to speak to what he was becoming.

### Mei-Lan Voss

Aldric’s former love. Barkeep of The Tideglass Inn. Caring, sharp, brunette, hair often in two buns. She represents warmth, ordinary life, and the future Aldric walked away from.

### Former Garrison Commander

Someone who knew Aldric as a soldier before he understood his divine nature. Could connect Aldric to the Clovis Concord, local defense, military duty, or old reports about his abilities.

### Pelorite Emissary

A celestial, cleric, or paladin who recognizes Aldric’s light. They may be hostile, sympathetic, confused, or politically constrained.

### Reformed Angel

One of the angels Aldric fought, now reformed or reforming. They remember the shroud and consider Aldric’s use of it a personal offense.

### Aeorian Analyst

Possibly Ellyanora or someone connected to her knowledge. They can explain Aldric’s divine nature in technical terms, stripping the mystery down in a way that may be useful but deeply uncomfortable.

## What I Want These Hooks To Do

I do not need Aldric to have a secret evil father, a dead sibling, or a random nemesis unless one of those becomes useful.

The hooks I care about most are the ones that pressure Aldric’s beliefs:

- He believes people are not property.
    
- He believes giving up is not responsibility.
    
- He believes strength should be used directly.
    
- He believes he can choose what to protect.
    
- He believes origin does not decide ownership.
    
- He believes standing firm is usually better than bending.
    

The best Aldric hooks are the ones that ask whether those beliefs still hold when gods, devils, old love, family, and party survival all pull in different directions.
